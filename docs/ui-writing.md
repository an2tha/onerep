# UI writing

Write for someone using the app, not someone evaluating a product pitch. State what a section contains, what a number means or what the user can do.

## Editing rules

- Use descriptive headings: “Hypertrophy handbook”, “How many sets?” and “Sleep and recovery”. Avoid slogans such as “without the guesswork”, “unlock your potential” and “give your progress a direction”.
- Remove decorative eyebrow labels. “YOUR GUIDE” above a guide title adds no useful information. A step counter is useful and should use ordinary sentence case.
- Prefer specific actions. Replace “Train with intent” with advice about reps, load or effort.
- Cut introductions that repeat the heading and conclusions that repeat the paragraph.
- Avoid stock contrasts such as “a range, not a magic number” and three-part slogans such as “Train. Observe. Adapt.” These are editorial problems here, not reliable evidence that a text was generated by AI.
- Explain technical language when the user needs it. Keep study terminology in the citation rather than the main instruction.
- Keep uncertainty where it changes a decision. Explain what the training bar measures once, rather than repeating a disclaimer under every number.
- Use lists for actual steps or choices. Do not force every explanation into three items or give every subsection the same sentence pattern.
- Read the copy aloud. Rewrite anything that sounds like an advertisement or that a trainer would need to translate into an instruction.
- Keep claims narrower than the evidence. Shorter copy must not turn a starting suggestion into an “optimal” prescription.

## Sources

[NN/g: Product-Specific GenAI Needs to Write for the Web](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/genai-write-for-the-web/) reports usability problems with long AI responses, irrelevant sales content and jargon. It recommends concise writing, useful formatting and putting the answer first. These findings concern usability, not a method for detecting AI authorship.

[Microsoft: Writing style for Windows apps](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/apps/design/style/writing-style) recommends familiar language, useful information first and removing unnecessary introductions. Text should help people understand what is happening and what to do next.

The restrictions on eyebrows, slogans and repetitive three-part phrasing are this app's editorial choices, informed by the user's feedback. Neither source establishes them as a universal test for AI-generated writing.
